# ALGOBIC: Content Strategy

Written 2026-07-30. Assumes **zero existing distribution** until told otherwise.

---

## 1. THE NICHE

**Not** "learn AI." That's the most crowded content category on the Indian internet: reportedly ~90% of learners already seek AI programmes, and every edtech pivoted there.

**Ours:** *"you could have built that."*

One sentence: **we take the thing people scroll past and show the whole path to building it, for people with no engineering background.**

Why this niche and not the adjacent ones:

| Adjacent niche | Why we don't |
|---|---|
| "Learn AI / prompt engineering" | Saturated. Commoditising to zero. |
| "AI news & tools roundup" | Infinite supply, zero retention, no moat |
| "Coding tutorials" | Speaks only to engineers. Kills 84% of the market |
| "Build in public as a founder" | Founder-centric. Wrong protagonist. |
| **"You could have built that"** | Broad audience, narrow promise, demonstrates taste, no prerequisite |

**The unfair fit:** our only real advantage is taste, and taste is invisible except through demonstration. So the content format and the competitive advantage are the same thing. That alignment is rare and we should exploit it ruthlessly.

---

## 2. THE MECHANISM: DEMONSTRATION, NOT EXPLANATION

Every other account in this space explains. We show.

The unit of content is **a real thing, built, with the whole path visible**, including where it broke and what the model got wrong. Explanation is copyable in an afternoon. A body of demonstrated work is not.

**Rule: never post advice we haven't executed.** Every post points at an artifact that exists.

## 3. ON YOUR FEAR-HOOK PLAN: the honest verdict

You proposed two hook types:

**(a) Fear**: *"you're 5 years behind if you don't know XYZ."*
**(b) Status**: *"know this and you already beat 90% of students."*

**⚠️ CORRECTED 2026-07-30 after primary-source verification. My original argument was weaker than I presented it.**

I originally argued (b) beats (a) mechanically, because DM shares outweigh likes for new-audience reach and fear content doesn't get forwarded.

**On verification, the DM-share claim is not in Metricool's actual 2026 study.** Their press release covers watch time, interaction multiples and saves. It contains no DM-share, completion-rate or like-weighting data. That figure came from secondary blogs attributing it to Metricool. **Unverified.**

And the second half ("people don't forward content about their own inadequacy") was always my reasoning, never a measured finding.

**So the honest position:**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Fear hooks under-perform on sends | **Unverified.** Plausible mechanism, no data. |
| Fear converts first action then churns | Established in behavioural literature, not measured for this niche |
| Status content gets forwarded as a flex | Reasoning, not data |
| Fear is off-voice for a "front door" brand | **Judgement, and it stands** (this one doesn't need data) |

**Revised verdict:** fear hooks are not banned and not capped by evidence. They're capped by *brand fit*, which is a taste call, not a finding. Run them as a real test rather than accepting my override.

**Test it properly:** 5 fear-hook posts vs 5 status-hook posts, same topics, same length. Compare sends-per-reach and follows-per-reach, not views. Whatever your own data says wins. If fear out-performs on sends, I was wrong and the cap comes off.

The one thing I'd hold regardless: never *"you'll be unemployed."* Not because it under-converts but because it's false (CS employability tested at 80%), and a brand built on a false premise is fragile in a way no engagement metric captures.

## 5. HOOK LIBRARY

The hook must survive the **first few seconds**. With an 8.5s average watch time, most of your view duration is decided almost immediately. *(The specific "50% drop before second four" figure is unverified: not in Metricool's study.)*

### Status (highest send rate)
1. "If you understand this, you're already ahead of most CSE graduates."
2. "Nobody in your college knows this yet."
3. "This is the difference between people who ship and people who watch."
4. "You don't need a CS degree for this. That's the entire point."
5. "Three years ago this needed a team. Watch."

### Utility (highest save rate)
6. "You've seen this app. Here's how it's actually built."
7. "The 40-minute version of the thing you saved last week."
8. "Copy this exactly. Change one thing. It's yours."
9. "Everyone stalls at step 3. Here's step 3."
10. "The prompt that does 80% of it."

### Demonstration (proves taste)
11. "This is what 40 minutes looks like."
12. "Same idea, built badly and built well. Spot the difference."
13. "I'm going to build this while you watch. No cuts."
14. "Every AI app looks the same. Here's why yours doesn't have to."
15. "This is why your build looks cheap." *(then fix it on screen)*

### The Catch (builds trust fastest)
16. "The AI got this wrong. Most people would have shipped it."
17. "This code runs. It's still wrong. Here's the tell."
18. "I asked for one thing. Look what it gave me."

### Curiosity
19. "What's the smallest useful thing you can build in one evening?"
20. "Nobody talks about the boring part, so I will."
21. "This took four tries. Here are the three that failed."

### Fear: cap at 10%
22. "There's a version of you in 2028 that wishes you'd started this year."
23. "The people getting hired next year are already shipping."

*Never:* "you'll be unemployed." It's false (CSE employability is above 80%), and false premises don't survive contact.

## 7. FORMAT AND LENGTH: the arithmetic

**Verified at primary source.** Metricool's 2026 Instagram study (**24,364,803 posts from 375,118 accounts**, Jan-Feb 2025 vs Jan-Feb 2026) puts average Reels watch time at **8.5 seconds, more than double YoY**. Reels generate **4x the interactions** of single-image posts; carousels get **9x the saves**. Single-image reach fell 21.96% YoY.

*Not in the study, despite being widely attributed to it:* completion-rate benchmarks, DM-share weighting, "50% drop off before second four," and "watch time is the strongest single signal." Those came from secondary blogs. **Treat as unverified.**

The 8.5s average still converts into a completion bar you have to clear. That arithmetic only needs the verified number:

| Length | Completion needed to hit 8.5s |
|---|---|
| 15s | ~57% |
| 30s | ~28% |
| 45s | ~19% |
| 60s | ~14% |
| 90s | ~9% |

**The production question is not "what length?" It's "what's the longest length where I can hold attention past 8.5 seconds with margin?"**

**Caveats, stated honestly:** 8.5s is a population average across 24M posts, not a threshold the algorithm enforces. We don't know whether watch time enters the ranking model raw or normalised by length. Working target, not a law. Circulating "50%+ for sub-15s / 30%+ for 30s+" benchmarks are unverified and are **not** from Metricool.

**Format allocation:**
| Format | Share | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reels 20-45s | 60% | **Verified:** 4x the interactions of single-image posts |
| Carousels | 25% | **Verified:** 9x the saves of single-image posts. The "how" content. |
| Stories | daily | **Verified:** story replies up 88% YoY. Send triggers, polls, behind-the-build. |
| Long-form (YouTube/LinkedIn) | 15% | Compounds and is searchable. Reels don't |

**Single-image posts: avoid.** Verified YoY decline: reach −21.96%, interactions −25.41%, engagement −45.98%.

**Every post still needs a send trigger**: an explicit reason to forward it to one person. *(The "sends outweigh likes" claim is unverified. The trigger costs nothing if the claim is wrong, so keep it.)*

## 8. CADENCE

**The largest number sustainable for twelve consecutive weeks with genuine differentiation. Floor of three per week**: below three the weekly iteration loop has no signal.

Not five-to-seven because that number was never derived from your actual capacity. Pick the number you can hold in week eleven, not week one.

## 9. EXPERIMENT ZERO: before trusting any of this

**Transfer validation.** Log **20 matched pairs** of Trial-Reel-vs-live performance before using Trial Reels as a testing framework. There is no established evidence that a hook winning among Trial variants also wins live, and if the ranking doesn't transfer we'd spend three months optimising against the wrong signal.

**Baseline before targets.** No invented performance numbers in this document and none allowed later. Ship the first ten posts, record actual hook rate / hold rate / completion / sends-per-reach, and beat *that*.

## 12. MEASUREMENT

Five numbers. Nothing else on the dashboard.

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Posts shipped / posts planned | 100% |
| Hook rate (3s) | `OUR-BASELINE`, set after 10 posts |
| Sends per reach | `OUR-BASELINE`, the growth metric |
| **CPQC**: cost per qualified conversation | Track from post one |
| Things actually shipped by other people | The only number that matters long-term |

**CPQC** = content spend ÷ people who reply with a specific stated goal. This is the loggable proxy for enrolments while nothing is for sale. **Log it from post one**, or in ninety days we'll have beautiful reach data and no idea whether any of it did anything.

**Pre-flight checklist** before publishing (a checklist, not a predictive score, and never a number out of 100):
☐ Hook survives second 4 · ☐ Send trigger is explicit · ☐ Points at a permanent page · ☐ An artifact exists · ☐ No banned words · ☐ Every claim has a name, date, or number
